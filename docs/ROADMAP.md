# Roadmap

What's left, in priority order. How each part works today is in [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md); the research behind it is in [FEASIBILITY.md](FEASIBILITY.md).

**Working now** (flown in Career, Battle of Stalingrad, 2026-10-25):
- Numpad 1–5: friendly positions, flight position, rejoin, enemy contacts, identify.
- Career callsigns, the game's own German and Russian pilot voices with a radio effect, and on-screen chat in exclusive fullscreen (through RTSS then; since 2026-10-28 through the radio's own `ingame` overlay, which the player has flown with).

**Built, not yet flown:** `Il2Radio.exe` (`src/Il2Radio.App`), the player's program. It is one self-contained file that asks for administrator rights itself and opens a window: flight status, a Help tab (what the radio is for, and when to use each call, for any air force), and settings saved to `settings.json` beside it. At start-up it checks rights and the `startup.cfg` outputs (asking before it changes them). During a mission it reminds the player to press LCtrl+R. See ARCHITECTURE.md, "The app". `Il2Radio.Diag` stays the developer console.

## 1. Joystick buttons
Today only the numpad works (`NumpadKeys`, polled with `GetAsyncKeyState`). The original brief asked for joystick buttons too.
- Read buttons without taking the device from IL-2: `Windows.Gaming.Input.RawGameController`, or DirectInput in non-exclusive background mode.
- Bindings go in the settings file (device + button → command, next to `keys`; `KeySettings`/`KeyBindings` today), with a "press a button to bind" helper.
- Never consume or remap input; IL-2 must still see every button.

## 2. Other theatres and mission types
Nothing is Stalingrad-specific, but nothing has been tested elsewhere either.
- **Maps:** Moscow, Kuban, Bodenplatte and Normandy. Check that lat/lon from ACMI and the cloud presets behave the same.
- **Voices:** US and British packs (the English voices' callsign clips are not mapped yet), and the Soviet side as the player (Russian voice pack, Allies coalition).
- **Mission types:** Quick Missions, scripted campaigns and PWCG. Check `_gen.Mission` vs other mission file names, AType 10 flight membership, and whether callsigns exist.
- **Aircraft vocabulary:** extend `AircraftTypes` for Bodenplatte and Normandy types not yet listed.

- ~~**Kneeboard plot**~~ (asked for 2026-10-28, so the player stops opening IL-2's map with O). Built 2026-10-28 as designed that day: a fixed sheet of the briefed route and home field, drawn by `ingame` as a third layer, with pencil marks only for what the radio has said (the player placed by an ally or his own landmark fix, then dead reckoning; mates where they reported; contacts where they were called), each with its age and fading. Three views on `keys.kneeboard` (`Num/`) and the radio menu, remembered in `settings.json`; placement presets (default `centre-right`) or a custom corner. See ARCHITECTURE.md, "Kneeboard", and the mockup [mockups/kneeboard-plot.svg](mockups/kneeboard-plot.svg). RTSS was removed in the same change, once the player had confirmed `ingame` held up in a flight; the window overlay stays as the fallback for a windowed IL-2 or a second screen. Follow-ups:
  - **Landmarks beyond airfields:** the Volga, the Don, the city, the larger towns and the railways, as a small list we write ourselves from public maps in mission metres via the `MapGrid` fit, checked against IL-2's map (O) before it is trusted. Until then a fix away from an airfield is "can't place myself".
  - **Tuning in a flight:** each view's size (30 % and 56 % of the screen height), the 2 % margin, the dead-reckoning growth (`DriftPerMetre` 0.10, `DriftPerMinute` 300 m) and its error (±6°, ±8 %), the fade (full for a minute, faint at eight) and how long marks stay.
  - **Escorts:** the sheet is the home field and five squares round it, because Career briefs an escort with only its landing point. The escorted flight's route, if Career's mission links it to ours, would make a better sheet.
  - Separate small and large placements, if one placement turns out not to suit both.

## Known gaps in the visibility model
Sun glare and bounces out of the sun; cockpit blind spots; terrain masking; combat awareness from log events (an aircraft shooting at you is obviously spotted); time of day uses mission start time plus recording time, not the mission clock the radio log now uses.
